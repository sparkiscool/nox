Flip swap: P3 steel core

The v4 swap station with the P3 pincer. The jaw is a base plus a screwed lid, each finger's arm bears up into the lid, and a 3 mm steel pin runs up the middle of each finger post. The posts print upright, so PETG is weakest exactly where they are loaded; the steel takes the pull and most of the bending. Shell, latch and holder are v4's, unchanged.

    The gate bar, from below

    State: latched

    Plan section 4.5 mm above the tip's load face, XT60 end. The finger post sits in the bar's slot next to the pivot. It moves 3.8 mm, the bar swings 14°, and the tip leaves the ledge and lines up with the release channel. The tail end is the same bar turned 180°.

    Finger post, cut through the middle

    The pin is a standard Ø3 × 20 mm steel dowel, pressed up from under the arm into a 2.95 mm hole (a drop of CA glue). It stops 3.6 mm under the post tip, so it never touches the gate bar. Once the finger sits in the jaw, the jaw floor stops the pin from sliding out.

    Numbers from the model

    Pincer strength, part by part

    Safety factor = PETG strength ÷ stress (45 MPa along the layers, 15 across; steel 400). Normal swap: 57 N pulling the XT60 apart, 15 N per finger on the bar. Misuse: the rail stalls pulling 150 N, the servo stalls pushing 41 N per finger (estimates). Target 2.0.
