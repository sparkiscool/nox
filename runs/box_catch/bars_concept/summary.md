## The design
Flat deck round the swap column. One NEMA 17 on a T8 x 2 lead screw (self-locking, thrust bearing) moves one carriage under the deck. Two phases, set by force (lost motion), no cam:
1. **Pads, x.** Two end pads 3 tall (top 1 under the ring tube centres, C) close along x at 1 : 1.361 nose : tail (the tail pad through a reversing compound pinion on the pad slide; the slide is on the carriage through a preloaded spring). The pad face is a profile along y: a 12-wide 120 deg notch for an Overseer tip node (its tip rods leave the node surface at |y| 7.9, C, so nothing wider touches the node alone), a relief beside it receding 14 (the rods run there at deck height), V wings |y| 28..80 at 18 deg, inner end 7.4 behind the apex, outer corners 9.5 ahead (they push an off-centre node inward; the Scout's straight end tube rests on their corners; the tip rods pass over them >= 0.6, C). The pads stop where the drone stops them: nose apex 203.2 for the Overseer (node 11.7 ahead), 97.1 for the Scout (tube on the corners); tail apex −224.6 / −80.2; parked 293.2 / −347.1; the ratio puts both receivers on the swap centre (C). Nose travel at the clamp 90 / 196 (C): the step count says which drone.
2. **Bars, y and yaw, hold-down.** The carriage runs 60 more against the pad spring; that relative motion, through a 2.3 : 1 lever, drives two side bars (50 long, centred between the side nodes, L-section) from park (|y| 258) onto the shared side tubes to a rigid stop, gap 0.5 (C). The bars wait because the side tubes taper 3.6 over 155 (a fixed gap fits at one x) and the Overseer's side rods leave the side nodes at 16 deg (a bar near a node before x is centred gets a rod across its top). The nodes ride the pad relief into the notches; the spring gives (stroke 80, used 71 / 62 median, 74 max, C). The lips (3 thick, 1.5 over the Overseer's tube top, edge 2 inside the tube line, 3 clear of its inner link, C) hold the 74 N head stall as a cantilever (5 MPa, E). Motor stall on the stop = seated; carriage travel 150 / 256 (C). Release: back out.

Scout: 6 mm printed sleeves on its side rods over x +-32 (DEFAULT, 2 x ~1 g) so one gap and one lip height serve both; its plane then −50.7 so both cartridges sit 7.0 above the deck (C; today 8.1). If the Scout adopts its 6/4 sized ring (frame model v2 lists all 4 bottom-loop rods) the sleeves fall away and the plane is −47.5 (Scout session).

## The check
Quasi-static push model on the current drones (`origin/main` 2bded7a0: the Overseer's v5 assembly, Scout layout v12 with the catch 5.0 forward; every low part of both assemblies, surface samples under the lip top; identical there to the dea0b51 geometry of the first sweeps, archived in `v1_park85/` and `v2_dea0b51/`): the drone is rigid on the deck; each 0.5 mm of carriage travel it is moved the least that resolves every contact (translation and yaw; in phase 2 the pad slide is a fourth unknown, sprung within its stroke). Bodies are orthogonal boxes in rotated frames, so sphere contacts are exact. A pushing face may only touch ring tubes, nodes and the tip-node structure; a lip touching anything, a body running under anything, a rod climbing a top edge more than 2 mm, or a landing on a parked body fails that landing. 9 x 9 offsets to +-80, yaw −15..15 (567 landings a drone, 6 chunks, ~60 min on this box). The Overseer's yaw limit is its tip geometry: a node in the notch / relief zone (|y| 6..28) is pushed outward by the notch corner while the other node on a wing is pushed inward, so the yaw grows until both nodes are on wings (~13 deg); wings cannot start further in (rod territory at deck height); 30 deg wings made it worse (unequal lever arms). The planned firmware commit gate (±30..40 mm, ±5 deg) sits inside the envelope, but the brief asks for the most error-tolerant catch and the research budget is ±80 / ±10 (the gate passes 30..60 % of first tries), so the Overseer's yaw envelope is the first thing the box catch session widens, in this order: (a) wings that start at the notch with the relief cut below the deck (the rods pass over a face whose top is at z −2.5; it then touches the node 2.5 below its equator, so it needs the real node bottom from the Overseer session); (b) a yaw-squaring pair outside the 16 deg rod zone that acts before the x clamp (low fingers at |y| 165+ on the side tubes' outer ends near the tail node, where the rods are 9 above them); (c) the Overseer's two tip rods 2 mm higher at the node (Overseer session). The x error is a bias when one pad has pushed the drone far (the sprung slide re-seats late): the Scout's is under 1.0 inside ±20, the Overseer's median 0.81 and max 4.06 there (Limits below); a second short cycle (back out 10, close) takes the rest (open).

## Alternatives weighed
- Ramps (concept E, `runs/box_catch/ramps_concept/`): passive; Scout ±20 / ±25, Overseer ±25, yaw ±2.5; no hold-down; needs μ ≤ 0.2 faces. A quarter of the room.
- Hook claws (the earlier direction, `runs/box_catch/hooks_concept/`): every claw rises outside the ring where both shells flare out at 16..20 deg, so the sweeps collide with shell rods under any offset; 12 claws on sprung links.

## Limits the model does not cover
- **Friction.** The push is rigid and frictionless: the drone is moved the least that resolves every contact. A node slides inward along an 18 deg wing only while the face friction is under tan 18 = 0.32 (C); PETG on steel reads 0.24..0.29 dry (research), carbon on PETG is not measured (E 0.25..0.4), so the wing faces get PTFE tape (research: on the pusher faces, not the lip). The drone's own drag (Overseer on TPU feet, mu 0.5..0.8, ~25 N; Scout on carbon, ~2 N, E) sets the pad spring preload and the carriage force, not the kinematics. A friction run is the box catch session's (the ramps' "mu <= 0.2" is the same kind of limit, stated for them above).
- **x error vs the swap.** The receiver lead-in captures +-3.3 and the arm tip uses 1.0 of it (dimensions.md System box swap). Inside +-20 the Scout's seated x error is under 1.0 (63 of 63 seat, max 0.97, C); the Overseer's is median 0.81 but max 4.06 (at yaws +-5 and +-10): of its 63 landings there 59 seat, 31 (49 % of all, 53 % of the seated) within 1.0 and 51 (81 % / 86 %) within 2.3, the lead-in left after the arm's 1.0; the rest, and the edge of the envelope (max 6.0 after one pad has pushed the drone 60..80 mm; the sprung slide re-seats the far node late), need a second short cycle (back out 10, close again), not modelled. Until that is in the check, the swap can use as is: Scout +-20 everywhere and +-40 for 154 of 175 landings (88 %) within 2.3; Overseer 51 of 63 landings (81 %) inside +-20.
- **Deck size.** The parks set the deck: x −355..301 (tail pad −347 minus 8, nose pad 293 plus 8) by y +-264 (bars at 258 plus 6), 656 x 528 (C), carriage travel 256 along x under it; the box's top slot takes it or the parks come in (each 10 mm of park is 10 mm of landing room). Mass E ~400 g with the motor.

## Rule checks (concept mode)
Cage first: nothing of the box stands inside the landing room above 11.5 (bars) / 3 (pads), both outside the seated cages; the drones' cages are untouched. Compact: the whole mechanism is under the deck plus 4 low pushers. Load path: the stall load goes lips → bars → guides → deck, not through the screw; the x clamp force is the spring (30 N preload, 0.5 N/mm, E), the carriage at the stall ~120 N (E) inside a NEMA 17 on T8 x 2 at 10..15 N·cm (research). Fit: the box session draws it; this check is kinematic. Mass: pads, bars, slide, lever, pinion, racks in PETG, 1 motor, 1 screw, 1 spring (E ~400 g with the motor).
