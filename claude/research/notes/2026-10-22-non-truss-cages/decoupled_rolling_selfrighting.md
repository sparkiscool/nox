# Decoupled, rolling and passively self-righting cages for small drones, plus the commercial state of the art

Context: Scout, about 520 g (E), 17 x 17 cm (P), about 110 mm tall (P), 3.5 in props on top of arms. The current cage is 3.0 mm carbon rods with TPU nodes. Hard constraints: no moving parts, passive self-righting (no turtle mode), survives plank hits, funnel dock.

Tag note: every number below is from the cited source unless it is marked (E) for an estimate or (derived) for my arithmetic on cited numbers.

## Q1. Flyability Elios 1/2/3 and Gimball: decoupled cage construction, mass, and what decoupling buys

### Takeaway
The only published numbers on decoupling come from the EPFL Gimball paper. In a 385 g robot, the 3-axis gimbal costs 56 g (15% of all-up mass, derived). That buys lower post-collision attitude disturbance and about 2x faster attitude control, because the inner frame's inertia is less than half of the whole robot's. Flyability itself dropped visible gimbal language after Elios 1. Elios 2 and Elios 3 use a carbon cage on thermoplastic-elastomer suspensions and lean on reversing motors and control for recovery, which includes recovering from flipping upside down. That is the "turtle" style of recovery Scout rules out.

### Cited Findings
- Gimball (EPFL LIS, 2014): total 385 g = inner frame 245 g + protective cage 84 g + gimbal 56 g. Cage radius 17 cm (about 34 cm across), pultruded carbon rods joined by 3D-printed ABS connectors. ([Briod et al., "A Collision Resilient Flying Robot", J. Field Robotics 2014, preprint PDF](https://infoscience.epfl.ch/record/190790/files/collision_resilient_flying_robots_LIS2014.pdf))
- Gimball cage survived numerous collisions at up to 3 m/s (about 50 cm free fall). Higher-speed impacts broke 3D-printed parts. ([Briod 2014 preprint](https://infoscience.epfl.ch/record/190790/files/collision_resilient_flying_robots_LIS2014.pdf))
- Decoupling test method: drops at 1 m/s and 1.8 m/s onto planes inclined 15°, 30°, 45° and 60° (1 mm linoleum added for friction). With the gimbal free, the inner frame's tangential coefficient of restitution was "significantly lower" than with the gimbal blocked in most cases. The exceptions were 15° at 1.8 m/s (both near frictionless) and 60° at 1.8 m/s (no improvement). Exact angular-rate values appear only in Fig. 8. ([Briod 2014 preprint](https://infoscience.epfl.ch/record/190790/files/collision_resilient_flying_robots_LIS2014.pdf))
- Inertia: inner frame 13.7 kg·cm² vs. whole robot 31.6 kg·cm². The authors estimate attitude control is about 2x faster than with a rigidly attached cage. Gimbal alignment tolerance target: 2.6 mm. Field test: 6 trials, 24 collisions, 470 m in about 5 min, forest at about 1.5 m/s. ([Briod 2014 preprint](https://infoscience.epfl.ch/record/190790/files/collision_resilient_flying_robots_LIS2014.pdf))
- EPFL press numbers differ slightly: "barely 370 grams", 34 cm diameter, "spherical and elastic" cage, stabilized by a "double carbon-fiber ring" gimbal. ([EPFL news, Gimball](https://actu.epfl.ch/news/gimball-a-crash-happy-flying-robot))
- Mechanism as described in the press: the cage absorbs the hit and converts it into rotation, so the inner drone stays level and keeps roughly its heading. Early Gimball price was about $25,000. ([IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/automaton/robotics/drones/flyabilitys-gimball-drone-exploring-ice-caves))
- The EPFL lab describes Gimball as having a "passively rotating protective cage". ([EPFL LIS AirBurr page](https://www.epfl.ch/labs/lis/research/completed/airburr-3))
- Elios 1 (2016): 0.75 kg, 40 cm, spherical carbon cage, about 10 min flight on 2800 mAh. ([IEEE Robots Guide](https://robotsguide.com/robots/elios)) Its cage is described as a carbon gimbal rotating on three axes around the drone, so it can roll along floors, ceilings and walls. ([RotorDrone via Magzter](https://www.magzter.com/hi/stories/Mens-Interest/RotorDrone/Flyability-Elios))
- Elios 2 (2019): under 1450 g including battery, payload and protection, fits a sphere under 400 mm. Cage is "carbon fiber with a soft coating", with "modular subcomponents" and "thermoplastic elastomer suspensions". Four 5 in props on "fast-reversing" motors. Payload has a load-limiting retractable mount against frontal shocks. Rated collision tolerance is uniform all around: "up to 3 m/s on flat objects, up to 1.5 m/s on sharp objects". No multi-axis cage gimbal is listed. ([Elios 2 spec sheet via xboom](https://www.xboom.in/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Copy-of-Eliso-2_compressed-1.pdf))
- Flyability framed the Elios 2 as moving collision tolerance "from passive mechanical protection to active control", with the intelligence in the "flight controller and motors". ([DroneLife 2019](https://dronelife.com/2019/04/30/dark-dirty-and-dangerous-flyabilitys-collision-tolerant-drone-has-upped-the-game-for-commercial-applications/)) The Elios 2 is "roughly twice the weight" of the original with the same flight time. Its reversible motors let it "automatically and near-seamlessly correct itself" after knocks. ([COPTRZ](https://coptrz.com/the-flyability-elios-2-caged-inspection-drone-has-lift-off/))
- Field anecdote: an Elios 2 hit a tunnel wall at full speed. One cage section broke and the drone kept working. ([Inside Unmanned Systems](https://insideunmannedsystems.com/elios-2-assessing-danger-in-a-cage/))
- Elios 3 (2022 to now): 48 cm wide x 38 cm high. Base 1900 ±10 g, with LiDAR 2350 ±15 g, MTOM 2500 g. Four 5 in props, flight over 12 min 30 s (base) or over 9 min (LiDAR). Cage is "carbon fiber with a soft coating", modular, "thermoplastic elastomer suspensions", with bottom and front openings. Claims: under 2 m/s frontal and under 2.4 m/s any direction for 100+ collisions without damage; under 3 m/s no structural damage; full-speed hits in ATTI/SPORT modes "destroy the drone". ([Elios 3 tech spec PDF](https://nexxis.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2022/06/20220519_E3_Tech_Spec_web_version.pdf))
- Elios 3 has "unique reversing motors" and "can recover from flipping upside-down without crashing". ([The Drone Girl](https://www.thedronegirl.com/2022/05/19/elios-3-drone-flyability/))
- Elios 1 and Elios 2 reached end of life; Elios 2 sales stopped after October 2024. ([DroneDJ](https://dronedj.com/2024/07/24/flyability-elios-drone-sales-end/)) Elios 3 got a UT (ultrasonic thickness) payload in 2024 with no cage change reported. ([DroneVideos](https://dronevideos.com/indoor-uav-pioneer-flyability-prepares-to-showcase-its-upgraded-elios-3-drone))
- Patent EP 17189515 (priority 5 Sep 2017, now US 11708160): the outer cage is coupled to the inner frame through a 2-axis gimbal. Couplings can be bearings, spring couplings, elastomeric joints or clock-spring joints allowing ±30 to 360°. A clutch, active or passive (magnetic discs, slips at about ¼ to ½ of max inner propulsion torque), can decouple the cage. ([US 11708160 PDF](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11708160)) This shows elastomer and clock-spring joints are recognized as limited-rotation "passive gimbal" substitutes.
- A 2023 survey classes gimbal cages as a category and states that the Gimball-derived design achieved commercial success. It gives no head-to-head quantified comparison of decoupled vs. fixed cages. ([De Petris et al., "Collision-Tolerant Aerial Robots: A Survey", arXiv 2212.03196](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.03196))

### Inferences
- What decoupling saves, scaled to Scout: at Gimball's ratios (gimbal 56 g on a 245 g inner frame, 23%), a Scout gimbal would cost about 100 to 120 g (E) and break the no-moving-parts rule. The benefit it buys is attitude rejection of tangential friction at contact. A low-friction cage skin (PTFE or soft-coated rods, rounded nodes) attacks the same friction term passively. Briod's data shows the benefit vanishes when contact is near frictionless (the 15° case), so a slick cage skin gets part of the gimbal's benefit for free.
- Flyability kept soft-coated carbon cages but replaced the gimbal with TPE suspension mounts plus active control and reversing motors. TPE suspensions between cage and frame are the closest passive analogue to decoupling that is fielded commercially. They are also directly compatible with Scout's TPU-node approach: make the cage-to-frame joints the compliant link, not just the rod nodes.
- Flyability's commercial rating ceiling is about 2 to 3 m/s on flat objects and 1.5 m/s on sharp ones for 1.4 to 2.5 kg drones. A swinging plank hitting Scout likely exceeds these (E). No commercial cage advertises sharp-edge tolerance above 1.5 m/s.

### Gaps
- No public Flyability statement explicitly says "we removed the gimbal" for Elios 2, or gives cage mass for Elios 2/3. Cage mass fraction for commercial cages is not published.
- The quantitative angular-rate results in Gimball Fig. 8 were not extractable as text.

## Q2. Rolling and hybrid cage robots: mass penalty and impact performance

### Takeaway
Rolling cages mostly buy endurance (4x to 15x range) rather than impact resistance. They come in two kinds. Decoupled ones (HyTAQ: the cage spins on a shaft) need a bearing axis, which is a moving part. Coupled ones (Quadrolltor, the CityU wheel frame, the US Army ring-cage patent) are fixed to the body, and the whole drone rolls by thrust. The coupled ones respect a no-moving-parts rule but tip the drone during rolling, so they need control rather than passivity to stay upright.

### Cited Findings
- HyTAQ (Kalantari and Spenko, ICRA 2013): a quadrotor inside a rolling cage, using the same four actuators. In ground mode it overcomes only rolling resistance. It travels 4x farther and operates almost 6x longer than aerial-only, and flies over obstacles. No mass or cage mass is published in the abstract. ([OpenAlex record of ICRA 2013 paper](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1109%2FICRA.2013.6631208); [IIT Robotics Lab](https://robots.iit.edu/multi-modal-locomotion.html))
- Quadrolltor (Jia et al., CityU HK, RA-L 2023): contrasts itself with prior bimodal quadrotors whose cylindrical rolling cages are "rotationally decoupled from the main body". It uses passively reconfigurable structures that tightly couple attitude to the rolling cage. Result: a large drop in cost of transport at low speed, giving 15x operational range. ([HKU Hub record](https://hub.hku.hk/handle/10722/355940))
- CityU passively reconfigurable wheel-frame quadrotor (about 2022): 156 g. The protective frame is the wheel (radius 90 mm), integrated with and not decoupled from the airframe. Materials: 2 mm carbon sheet and 2 mm carbon rods. Two props on passive revolute joints use rubber bands (about 196 N/m per joint). Rolls at about 2.8 m/s and through a 10 cm gap vs. 18 cm flight width. Roll-to-fly transition succeeded 100% above about 5.35 rad/s. ([Jia et al. PDF](https://ris.bme.cityu.edu.hk/wp-content/papercite-data/pdf/jia2022a.pdf))
- US 12263969 B1, "Wall rolling UAV with ring cage" (US Government interest): a ring cage fixed to the body by six attachments, parallel to the prop plane. The whole vehicle revolves about its z-axis to roll along walls, with the contact point instantaneously at rest. The text explicitly contrasts this with prior art where the cage rolls around the UAV. Six tilted props (15 to 30°). ([US 12263969 PDF](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12263969))
- JPL Rollocopter: a quadrotor on two passive wheels that flies over obstacles, with up to 10x greater range than an aerial drone. Completed in 2020. ([JPL](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/robotics-at-jpl/subterranean-rollocopter/))
- Arizona hybrid rolling/flying sphere (IROS 2021): uses a flywheel to isolate the camera from terrain shocks, runs PX4. No mass numbers in the abstract. ([U. Arizona record](https://experts.arizona.edu/en/publications/a-highly-maneuverable-hybrid-energy-efficient-rollingflying-syste/))
- Japan MoD spherical drone (Fumiyuki Sato, TRDI, 2011): 350 g, 42 cm sphere, about 110,000 yen (about $1,400) of off-the-shelf parts. A single prop plus control flaps inside an open spherical shell. Hovers 8 min, up to 60 km/h. Can land on irregular surfaces, roll or "bounce along the ground", and survives wall knocks and tumbles. ([Design News](https://www.designnews.com/aerospace/japan-defense-ministry-spins-flying-spherical-robot); [Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2011-08-japanese-inventor-sphere-drone.html))
- Survey data on bimodal designs: Drivocopter, 5.1 kg, uses spherical wheels as protection. ([De Petris survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.03196))

### Inferences
- The Japan MoD sphere is worth noting for Scout: 350 g of drone fills a 42 cm sphere. Its passive righting comes from a single heavy core at the bottom of an axisymmetric sphere (a roly-poly), not from gyros. The source credits gyros for recovering in flight only.
- No source quantifies impact performance of a rolling cage vs. a fixed cage. Rolling cages are not designed for side hits by moving objects like a plank.
- For a funnel dock, a fixed ring or sphere shell that rolls with the body is compatible. A free-spinning cage would arrive in a random orientation, which is harder to dock on pure geometry.

### Gaps
- HyTAQ cage mass and material, and CAROLLINE (KIST caged rolling drone) specs, could not be retrieved (KIST site blocked).

## Q3. Passive self-righting: shape rules, mass distribution, and a simple check

### Takeaway
With a non-uniform mass (any real drone), passive self-righting is easy and needs no gömböc. If the hull's top and sides are curved with centers of curvature above the CoM, and only the bottom face is flat with the CoM's vertical projection inside it, the bottom is the only stable rest. Every polyhedral cage facet other than the bottom must have the CoM's perpendicular foot fall outside the facet. This can be checked in 2D on paper or in 3D with a short support-function script over the CAD mesh.

### Cited Findings
- A sphere with an off-center mass is mono-monostatic (one stable, one unstable equilibrium). Roly-poly/Weeble toys work because pushing raises the CoM and produces a righting moment. The hard gömböc problem only exists for homogeneous bodies. ([Wikipedia, Gömböc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6mb%C3%B6c))
- Physical gömböc tolerance is about 10⁻³ (0.1 mm on a 10 cm body), which is impractical for a cage. ([Wikipedia, Gömböc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6mb%C3%B6c)) A 2026 computational study confirms this. A sphere becomes monostatic with only 5% bottom ballast. Ellipsoids and capsules were already monostable unballasted in its sim. Cylinders and cubes never became monostable even with 30% bottom-weighted mass. The gömböc needs better than 0.01% shape accuracy. The method counts local minima of CoM height over about 5,000 sampled resting directions ("Equilibrium Count Score"). ([Couey, arXiv 2604.17095](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.17095)) Single author, simulation only; treat as indicative.
- Turtles: high-domed tortoise shells have height, width and curvature close to optimal for self-righting, needing only small head or leg movements. Flat aquatic turtles are stable on their backs and must lever over with their necks (30 shells, 17 species; Proc. R. Soc. B, 2007). ([LiveScience](https://www.livescience.com/1967-turtle-tipping-tricks-revealed.html)) The Indian star tortoise has "only one stable configuration: on its feet", but its uneven density (lungs high in the dome) makes it less ideal than a gömböc. ([Natural History mag](https://nhmag.com/biomechanics/10309/the-living-g-mb-c))
- Robot shell design rule (King and Revzen, ICRA 2024, hexapod): each coronal shell edge is placed so its perpendicular bisector is tangent to a circle of radius r centered on the CoM. Every flat face then produces a righting moment about the CoM. The maximum incline it still self-rights on is atan(r/d), where d is the distance from the face midpoint to the tangency point. Material above the CoM was reduced to keep the CoM low. Results: about 16° theoretical incline tolerance; righted from all 28 face trials; 15/15 righted after 1 m ledge falls. ([King and Revzen 2024](https://www.birds.eecs.umich.edu/assets/pdf/2024-King-Revzen-ICRA.pdf))
- Drone self-righting frame patents (Gaofei Yan and James Dees; priority CN 2010-07-23; US 8528854 granted 10 Sep 2013; continuations incl. US 10112694, US 2023/0060888): two or more intersecting elliptical vertical frames (wider than tall), a flattened bottom, and ballast (battery and electronics) low on the axis. Because an oblate top would otherwise be stable inverted, a spherical-tipped protrusion on top makes the inverted pose a single-point unstable contact. Condition: the protrusion height Z is chosen so the CoM horizontal offset X exceeds Y, the distance from the protrusion contact to the frame contact line. Stable when the weight vector passes inside the triangle of three contact points. Height is no more than 2x width. Coaxial counter-rotating rotors. ([US 8528854 abstract, Justia](https://patents.justia.com/patent/8528854); [US 10112694 PDF](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10112694); [US 2023/0060888, Justia](https://patents.justia.com/patent/20230060888))
- MIT-style monostatic capsule patent: uses a gömböc shape or two portions with a density ratio of at least 2.5:1; self-rights from 90° in water in 0.05 s or less. ([US 11607390 PDF](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11607390)) Shows density ratio as the design lever.
- AirBurr (EPFL): V2 (2010) used gravity-based self-recovery. Samurai/V8 (2012) used active spring-loaded carbon legs, righting within 25 s in 100% of flat-ground trials, but struggled on slopes over 10° and on gravel. ([IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/airburr-mav-can-now-selfright-is-utterly-unstoppable); [EPFL LIS](https://www.epfl.ch/labs/lis/research/completed/airburr-3))
- Tensegrity drones (Zha and Mueller, Berkeley) are not passive. They use an active re-orientation controller with bi-directional ESCs to rotate face to face until reaching a takeoff face. ([arXiv 2211.12045](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.12045v1); [HiPeR Lab PDF](https://hiperlab.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/TensegrityAerialVehicle.pdf))
- Click-beetle-inspired righting is an active spring-loaded hinge jump, not shape-based. ([R&D World 2017](https://www.rdworldonline.com/click-beetles-inspire-design-of-self-righting-robots/))
- Commercial "self-righting" in small drones is turtle mode (motor reverse spin). DJI Avata 2 has Turtle Mode ([Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/tech/dji-avata-2-fpv-drone-price-release-date-features)), as do Skydio R10 ([Skydio FAQ](https://www.skydio.com/r10/faq)) and Elios 3 ([Drone Girl](https://www.thedronegirl.com/2022/05/19/elios-3-drone-flyability/)). No current commercial small drone found uses a purely passive shape-righting cage.

### Inferences (derived; standard rigid-body statics, not from a single source)
- 2D rule for any side profile (do it for both the front and side views of Scout's convex hull, cage plus props):
  1. Equilibria are hull points where the normal passes through the CoM G.
  2. Vertices (corners, rod nodes) are never stable rests. Height of G is a local maximum there.
  3. A flat face is a stable rest if the perpendicular from G lands inside the face. Only the bottom face may satisfy this.
  4. A curved arc of radius ρ is a stable rest if the distance from G to the contact point is less than ρ (G between the arc and its center of curvature). So every arc other than the bottom must have its center of curvature closer to the arc than G is. For the top dome, its center must sit above G.
- Spherical-cap check for Scout: a single sphere of R = 85 mm (half of 170 mm) with its top at 110 mm puts the center at 25 mm above the bottom plane, with a bottom flat radius of about 81 mm (derived). G must then be below 25 mm, which is unrealistic for a quad with props on top of the arms (G likely 35 to 50 mm, E). Fixes:
  - (a) A tighter top dome (smaller R, center at or above G) blended into side arcs with ρ less than the distance from G to the side (about 85 mm). Rounded sides are automatically unstable if ρ is under 85 mm.
  - (b) A Yan-Dees style top protrusion (a "pip" or apex node).
  - (c) Moving the battery down. Ballast is the strongest lever: the computational study shows a sphere goes monostable with 5% bottom ballast.
- Righting moment magnitude: for a spherical top of center C with G a distance e below C, the righting moment at tilt θ is m·g·e·sin θ. For 0.52 kg and e = 20 mm, the peak is about 0.10 N·m (derived). That is small, so grip and edge snagging matter, and a rod node digging into dirt can hold it. Keep top nodes smooth and avoid flat top facets.
- Rod-and-node polyhedral cages are faceted. Every top or side facet whose foot-of-perpendicular from G lies inside it is a stable "turtle" rest. This is likely why a dome-ish truss can come to rest upside down. Apply the King-Revzen tangent-circle rule to set facet orientations, and use atan(r/d) as the slope margin.
- 3D check, about 20 lines of Python on the STL/hull vertices: for about 5,000 unit directions n, compute h(n) = max over vertices of (v − G)·n (the support function, equal to the height of G when resting with outward normal n downward). Find local minima on the sphere of directions. Exactly one minimum (bottom) means passively monostable. The number of extra minima and their basin size quantifies the risk. This mirrors the ECS method in [arXiv 2604.17095](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.17095).
- Static monostability does not guarantee dynamic righting after a tumble on rubble or a slope. It needs margin (atan(r/d)) and smooth contact.

### Gaps
- No published drone (as opposed to toy or patent) with measured passive self-righting success rate found, beyond AirBurr V2's qualitative "gravity-based" recovery.
- The Domokos/Várkonyi turtle paper's numeric height-to-width ratios were not retrievable (PMC blocked).

## Q4. Commercial indoor and confined-space drones 2024-2026: protection materials, mass, impact claims, non-truss protection

### Takeaway
The commercial field splits into two camps. Flyability uses big soft-coated carbon truss cages on TPE suspensions with quantified 1.5 to 3 m/s ratings. Everyone else at Scout's scale (Skydio R10, Liberaware IBIS2, Cleo Dronut, DJI Avata 2, cinewhoops) uses integrated ducts or blade guards, with no published impact ratings and turtle mode or active control for recovery. The Dronut and IBIS2 are the clearest successful non-truss protection: a fully enclosed ducted carbon body, and a flexible polycarbonate shell.

### Cited Findings
- Cleo Robotics Dronut X1 (2021): 15 oz (about 425 g), 6.5 x 4 in, two coaxial counter-rotating props inside an enclosure. Airframe "made from light, rugged carbon fiber composite materials", 12 min flight, LiDAR. Claims it can "collide and continue flying" without harm, but gives no speed. ([UAV Coach](https://uavcoach.com/dronut-x1/)) An earlier Dronut was 10.5 oz, 5.5 in diameter x 3.3 in, carbon body, 15 min. ([The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/cleo-robotics-dronut-compact-drone-dangerous-jobs/)) Dronut DD1 was announced July 2025 with "fully enclosed propellers"; no specs given. ([Unmanned Systems Technology](https://www.unmannedsystemstechnology.com/2025/07/cleo-robotics-introduces-dronut-dd1-for-advanced-indoor-and-confined-space-operations/))
- Liberaware IBIS2: 243 g, about 20 cm, body of "flexible, lightweight polycarbonate". Attitude control lets it keep flying horizontally after a side-wall hit ("side crash"). Enters 30 cm holes and 50 cm ducts, 11 min flight. Used by JR East and at the 2024 Noto earthquake. ([Japan Gov "Highlighting Japan" July 2024](https://www.gov-online.go.jp/hlj/en/july_2024/july_2024-02.html))
- Skydio R10 (announced Sep 2025): 785 g, 10.4 x 10.3 x 2.4 in, enclosed props with "strong propeller guards". No quantified impact rating. Turtle Mode flips it upright. NightSense obstacle avoidance, up to 20 min. ([Skydio R10 FAQ](https://www.skydio.com/r10/faq); [DroneXL](https://dronexl.co/2025/09/19/skydio-unveils-new-drones-for-indoor/))
- DJI Avata 2 (2024): about 377 g, 185 x 212 x 64 mm. ([Clifton Cameras spec PDF](https://www.cliftoncameras.co.uk/uploads/specifications/dji-avata-2-drone-only-specifications.pdf)) Integrated prop guards and Turtle Mode. No crash ratings published. ([Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/tech/dji-avata-2-fpv-drone-price-release-date-features))
- GEPRC CineLog35 V2 (3.5 in cinewhoop, same prop class as Scout): 285.8 g (O3 configuration, battery excluded), 142 mm wheelbase, carbon plates plus 7075 aluminum columns, and an "injection protection frame" (molded duct). ([GetFPV](https://www.getfpv.com/geprc-cinelog-35-v2-3-5-hd-cinewhoop-drone-w-dji-o3-6s.html))
- Voliro T (contact-NDT, spec sheet v3.2, June 2025): 3.7 kg without batteries, 6.0 kg MTOW, 118 x 109 x 34 cm, "bumpers" listed but no material or collision claims. ([Voliro spec sheet](https://cdn.voliro.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Voliro-T-Spec-sheet-v3.2.pdf)) Not a collision-tolerant design.
- Nolvis X1 (sold as Terra Xross 1 alternative): 1800 g, 36 x 31 x 21 cm, accessory cage $400, "contact-rated design", replaceable parts, no ratings. ([DSLRPros](https://www.dslrpros.com/products/terra-xross-1-indoor-inspection-drone); [Dronefly](https://www.dronefly.com/products/terra-xross-one-cage))
- Emesent Hovermap is a payload. The only cage material found is for Spot (the robot dog), not drones. ([Mining Technology](https://www.mining-technology.com/products/spot-cage-for-hovermap/))
- ARIX Technologies makes pipe-crawling robots, not drones. ([Creative Destruction Lab](https://creativedestructionlab.com/companies/arix-technologies/))
- Third-party cages on non-designed drones: a Mavic 2 cage halved flight time (about 30 to 15 min) and forced obstacle sensing off. ([COPTRZ](https://coptrz.com/blog/cage-match-the-cheap-fix-vs-elios-2/))

### Inferences
- At 243 to 785 g (Scout's class), no commercial product uses a gimbal or rolling cage. All use body-integrated guards or ducts (Dronut, R10, Avata 2, IBIS2 polycarbonate). This supports a non-truss, shell-or-duct protection direction for Scout.
- None of these publish impact speeds, and all small ones rely on turtle mode or active control. Passive self-righting would be a genuine differentiator for Scout.
- A ducted or shell body has a smooth convex hull with no snag nodes, which helps both funnel docking and the passive-righting geometry in Q3.

### Gaps
- No teardown data found on duct or shell materials and thicknesses for R10, Dronut or IBIS2, beyond "carbon composite", "polycarbonate" and "guards".
- No commercial plank- or swinging-object impact tests published by anyone.

## Q5. 2025-2026 research and products on new cage concepts

### Takeaway
Recent work moved from rigid cages toward compliant frames and arms (FlexiQuad 2025, HoLoArm 2025-26, DART 2025). These report 4.4 to 7.7 m/s survival and about 30% peak-force reduction, typically with TPU or elastic elements, and no rotating parts. Older benchmarks still define the ceiling: tensegrity at 6.5 to 7.8 m/s and 11.7 m/s drops. No 2025-2026 paper on a passive self-righting cage for quadrotors was found.

### Cited Findings
- FlexiQuad (Girardi, Maquignaz, Mintchev, EPFL; arXiv 2511.05426, Nov 2025): a 405 g soft-frame quad about 3 orders of magnitude more compliant than conventional, yet acrobatic. About 4x the collision resilience of a rigid design; survives 5 m/s frontal impacts undamaged; glancing-collision destabilizing forces cut by 39x; squeezes through gaps 70% of nominal width. Optimal frame softness 0.006 to 0.77 N/mm across 20 to 3000 g models. ([arXiv 2511.05426](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.05426v1))
- HoLoArm (Pham, Eschmann, Zhou, Ojeda Olarte, Loianno, Ho; RA-L accepted Dec 30 2025, arXiv 2605.25790 May 2026): dragonfly-nodus-inspired compliant arm hinges. TPU ligaments and joints, PLA arms, carbon/PLA prop guards, plus a sliding linear bearing (written "LM88U" in the source, probably LM8UU) for axial give (note: this is a moving part). 970 g drone. Drop impacts 4.4 to 7.7 m/s (survival claimed to 7.6 m/s). Peak force 11.65 vs. 16.69 N at 1 m, 14.07 vs. 19.21 N at 1.5 m, 16.52 vs. 23.99 N at 3 m (about 27 to 31% lower, derived). The rigid frame broke at 3 m. Recovers in 0.27 to 0.97 s. ([arXiv 2605.25790](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.25790))
- DART (arXiv 2504.18780, Apr 2025): 1.3 kg dual-stiffness frame with servo-locked arms (active), PLA plus 5 mm aluminum rods. Contact time 46 vs. 177 ms (rigid vs. flexible) at 0.05 m drops. Stiffness 5500 vs. 750 N/m on concrete. ([arXiv 2504.18780](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.18780v1))
- Tensegrity benchmarks (Berkeley): 252 g vehicle survives 6.5 m/s collisions ([ar5iv 2003.03417](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2003.03417)). A 300 g icosahedron version has a 95 g shell (6 mm OD carbon rods, braided nylon). It survived a 7 m drop at 11.7 m/s (failed at 14.4 m/s when a string snapped) and a 7.8 m/s concrete wall hit, while a 250 g prop-guarded quad fractured from 3.25 m at 8.0 m/s. ([HiPeR Lab PDF](https://hiperlab.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/TensegrityAerialVehicle.pdf))
- EPFL dual-stiffness frame (2017, magnet-detach): 0.3 mm fiberglass frame on 4 magnetic joints, protection about 4.66 g (9% of mass), 50+ drops from 2 m without permanent damage. ([NCCR Robotics PDF](https://nccr-robotics.ch/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/32-Insect-Inspired-Mechanical-resilience-for-multicopters.pdf); [De Petris survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.03196))
- Survey numbers for scale: a 25 g quad with a 3 g frame survives 4 m/s; an arthropod-inspired soft design under 250 g reaches 7 m/s; a compliant-arm quad 5.9 m/s; origami 1 to 2 m/s. ([De Petris survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.03196))

### Inferences
- For Scout (about 520 g E), recent literature suggests compliant joints (TPU) at the frame-to-guard interface give about 30% lower peak force and survival in the 5 to 7.7 m/s range. Tensegrity is the proven high-speed outlier but needs an active re-orientation controller.
- A combination fits all of Scout's constraints and uses only fielded elements: a smooth convex shell or duct hull (Dronut/IBIS2 style), Flyability-style TPE suspension mounts, and a Q3 monostatic geometry (top center of curvature above the CoM, low battery).

### Gaps
- No 2025-2026 paper on passive self-righting caged quadrotors found. The BUAA "compact spherical cage" collision-mitigation paper could not be retrieved (permission timeout at buaa.globalimpact.cn).
- No measured data on swinging-plank (moving-object) impacts vs. drones found; all tests are drone-into-wall or drop.
