Cage v14: corner spokes and a crown ring

Same lower half, same 29 rods of 3.0 carbon, about the same mass. The crown changes from eight spokes into a hub to four corner spokes tied by a square ring over the props, the shoulder ring rises 60 to 68, and only the corner and nose columns stay. In the frame model that doubles what a top hit takes and cuts the paths a plank edge can reach a prop by more than five times.

What changed and why

Crown

Eight radial spokes left a wedge-shaped gap over every prop. Four corner spokes with a square ring at 40% of their length put a rod straight over each prop disk and make the top a braced frame instead of a fan. Top-hit capacity 0.15 to 0.29 J (F).

Columns

Tail and side columns go; corners carry the drum and the nose column keeps the camera pads. The side and tail flats become 200 mm through-rods. Printed mass about 4 g lower (E), three more small printed parts.

Shoulder ring

Rises from z 60 to 68. The drum window grows to 35 mm tall, still too small for a 38 mm plank edge, and the camera gets a taller view. The crown ring sits about 30 mm above the swept disks; nearest rod to a prop 20 to 26.5 mm (F).

What it costs

  • Weakest plank-edge span drops 4%: 0.529 to 0.508 J (F), now the 200 mm shoulder flats, which bow 8.6 mm at that load, still 23 mm above the swept disks.
  • Upside down, the blip has to lift the CG 46.9 mm instead of 43.0: 0.24 J vs 0.22 J (E). Same top height, z 85.
  • 20 printed parts instead of 17: four crown-ring nodes on the spokes and two small side nodes, against three columns removed (tail and both sides).

Before it prints

  • The new parts (hub, 4 crown nodes, corner and nose columns at z 68) need the topology run and solid_check.py; this is the skeleton only.
  • D21 rod bend test sets every energy number; D20 motor test confirms the blip still clears 0.24 J.
  • A lower crown (z 74) scores better still, 0.34 J top hits and 11 mm shorter, but needs a 0.30 J blip. Hold it until D20.
