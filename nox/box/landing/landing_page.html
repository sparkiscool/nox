Three feet, three grooves

The new Overseer cage has no funnel-shaped bottom, and the Scout will get its own cage. The one thing both drones share is the bottom opening ring. So the box catches that ring: three short feet under it, each tipped with a bought ball-transfer unit, drop into three V-grooves on the box top. Every drop inside the capture rolls into the same seat to the millimetre; nothing on the box moves.

The shared interface, to scale

Plan, ring frame: x forward along the receiver, origin at the receiver centre, grid 50 mm. The ring is today's Overseer cage v3 opening (8 nodes round the swap zone, receiver + 30 a side). Feet F1 and F2 sit on the front-diagonal ring tubes, F3 on the rear end tube; the Overseer's CG is 91 mm ahead of the receiver, inside the triangle pack in and pack out (+119). Each groove points at the triangle's centroid (a Maxwell coupling), so the seat is fixed in x, y and yaw. The swap hole stays open between the blocks; the down camera and flow look through it, and the feet are 70° or more off their axes, out of view.

Across a groove

A ball tip in a V touches both flanks, and both contact forces pass through the ball's own centre. That is why it centres to zero: an off-centre foot sits on one flank, which pushes it back with a force that does not shrink near the bottom, and the drone cannot rock its way out of it. The block tops stay 5 mm under the ring node bottoms, so nothing but the feet ever touches.

Every configuration run

What was weighed

3 feet + 3 V-grooves, ball tips (DEFAULT)

Kinematic seat, exact every time inside the capture, static PETG blocks. Costs three ~{{H}} mm feet under each cage with a ball-transfer unit in each tip (~+30 g a drone, E; they replace the four TPU floor feet) and a landing gate of ±25 mm, ±5°, which the down camera's tag landing has to meet.

Same, PTFE glide tips

No rolling part in the foot, and the Overseer catches out to ±40 mm. The Scout jams: two feet seat first and the third, carrying only 27% of its weight, cannot drag them along their grooves. Even at 40° flanks with 55 mm feet the Scout holds only ±10 mm.

Tray, rolling nodes

Flat floor inside the ring, 10° walls outside, a ball transfer in each of the 8 ring nodes (+~60 g a drone, E). Centres only with rolling friction, and then has no damping: the drone rolls across and drops a node into the swap hole.

One shallow cone

Fits under today's cage (its underside rises only 7° from the ring) but a wide ring on a cone just tilts when off-centre: the centring force falls to about W·e/1000, so it stops 30 mm off even rolling.

Not run

A collar round the whole cage (it would bear on shell facets that differ between the drones, and be ~900 wide); pushers that sweep the drone to centre (an old design, out unless Spark asks).

What each drone carries

  • Both: the same bottom ring, fixed to the receiver: today's Overseer cage v3 opening. The Scout's cage takes it as its own opening, so the Scout's bottom grows to about 372 x 229.
  • Both: three feet at F1 (150, 58.4), F2 (150, −58.4), F3 (−176, 0), {{H}} mm below the ring node bottoms, each a printed leg (TPU where it meets the floor) with a bought ball-transfer tip; nothing else of the drone below the ring node bottoms.
  • Both: a down camera looking through the opening (Spark: two cameras); it reads an AprilTag on the box for precision landing and commits only inside ±25 mm, ±5°, else climbs and retries.
  • Overseer: CG inside the feet triangle pack in (+91) and pack out (+119); its cage needs no other change.
